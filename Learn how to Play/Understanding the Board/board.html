Understanding the Board

The Catan board is an island of hexagonal tiles surrounded by a frame of sea pieces. It's different every game if you set it up randomly.

Terrain Tiles

There are 19 land tiles. Each one produces a resource when its number is rolled.

TerrainProduces
HillsBrick
ForestLumber
PastureWool
FieldsGrain
MountainsOre
DesertNothing (the robber starts here)

Number tokens

Each tile (except the desert) has a number from 2 to 12. When the dice total matches, the tile pays out. The dots under each number show how likely it is: 6 and 8 come up the most, while 2 and 12 are rare. Build next to tiles with lots of dots to get more resources.

Harbors

Harbors sit on the sea frame. Without one, you can swap four of the same resource for one of another kind. If you own a settlement or city on a harbor, your rate improves, down to as low as two for one with a matching resource harbor.

Where you can build

Roads

Go on the edges between tiles and must connect to your own roads or buildings.

Settlements and cities

Go on the corners where tiles meet. A new settlement must connect to your road and sit at least two road spaces from any other settlement.

First-game setup

  • Join the sea pieces into a frame, then fill it with the 19 land tiles.
  • Place the number tokens, and put the robber on the desert.
  • Each player picks a color and takes their roads, settlements, and cities.
  • Players place two settlements and two roads, and the second round is placed in reverse turn order.
  • Your second settlement gives you a starting card from each tile it touches.

For a beginner game, use the fixed layout shown in the rulebook instead of a random one.
